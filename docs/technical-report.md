# Experimental methods and limitations

## From a stereo pair to a control observation

The live rig has two parallel 960 × 540 RGB cameras with 80° horizontal field of view, 0.32 m baseline, and matched timestamps. For the simulator pinhole model, fx = fy = W / [2 tan(FOV/2)], or about 572 px. The left camera sits 1.6 m forward, 0.16 m left and 1.5 m above the ego origin. CARLA supplies the mounting transforms and optics. OpenCV computes distortion/rectification maps from these calibrated parameters. A separate board-corner experiment tests recovery of an unknown relative pose with known intrinsics.

StereoSGBM searches a 64-disparity range using a 5 px block and a left-right consistency check. Its output is divided by 16 to obtain pixel disparity d. For d > 0, optical depth Z = fx B / d. Invalid pixels remain missing. Inverse projection gives X = (u − cx)Z/fx and Y = (v − cy)Z/fy. For this parallel left camera the ego forward/right/up coordinates are (Z + 1.6, X − 0.16, 1.5 − Y). The first-order sensitivity |∂Z/∂d| = Z²/(fx B) explains why far-range estimates are particularly sensitive to correspondence errors.

Obstacle localization filters points within a two-metre corridor, above the expected road surface. It scans range slices from near to far and requires an 18-pixel connected component. The median component coordinates estimate a visible surface. The two published pairs support a direct reproduction. The [sample coordinates](../results/carla_td3/sample_points.json) give the estimated obstacle surface in both optical and ego frames. This is a corridor heuristic for this camera mount. Hills, roadside clutter and a changing camera pitch are important failure cases.

For evaluation the aligned left depth sensor is decoded from RGB bytes as 1000(R + 256G + 65536B)/(2²⁴ − 1). Dense error statistics pool pixels in each reference-depth band. Coverage is the number of finite predictions divided by the number of eligible reference pixels. Obstacle range uses a separate reference, the nearest forward coordinate of the parked vehicle’s bounding-box vertices in the left optical frame. Box extent and visible bodywork differ. The reported range error includes that discrepancy. Only the RGB estimator supplies obstacle observations to control. The controller uses camera-derived lane geometry and ground-plane visual odometry.

## Geometry checks at three levels

1. **Calibration.** Fit 36 synthetic stereo chessboard poses with 0.1 px corner noise and fixed camera intrinsics. Evaluate vertical alignment on 12 unseen poses. The baseline fit was 0.31985 m for a 0.32002 m rig. Rotation error was 0.0063°, and the held-out vertical residual was 0.096 px median and 0.278 px at the 95th percentile. These are projected corner measurements.
2. **Known planes.** At 4, 8 and 16 m the original 480 × 240 synthetic plane test gave MAEs of 0.056, 0.145 and 0.772 m. Ten images per range and a single warm-up per range were used. About 86.7% of pixels had valid disparity. This rig used fx = 360 px and B = 0.24 m.
3. **Urban scene.** The live experiment uses independently rendered left and right views. Pixel coverage and error by reference-depth band are recorded in each run. Empty disparity is kept invalid. Sparse range estimates describe the frontmost coherent surface in the driving corridor. [TD3 run metrics](../results/carla_td3/metrics.json)

## Tracking task and camera observations

The Gymnasium simulator uses a forward-Euler kinematic bicycle with dt = 0.1 s and wheelbase L = 2.6 m. Position rates are v cos ψ and v sin ψ, yaw rate is v tan δ / L and speed rate is a. Normalized actions map to steering δ = 0.52uₛ radians and acceleration a = 3uₐ m/s², with speed clipped to [0,9] m/s. Each 40 m route is straight, sinusoidal, or a Gaussian lateral bypass of a stationary obstacle. Each episode begins at rest. Lateral position, heading, obstacle position, texture and bypass side vary with seed.

### What reaches the learner

The Gymnasium simulator renders a calibrated, rectified grayscale pair (192 x 112, focal length 115 px, baseline 0.32 m) from a textured road and painted boundaries. A textured vertical obstacle panel has parallax in the two images. Only the renderer and vehicle dynamics use the simulated pose. The perception frontend accepts two images and a timestamp. It extracts paired lane stripes on the ground plane, fits the near tangent, interpolates the painted center at the lookaheads and obtains a foreground range and bearing from SGBM disparity. The obstacle bypass is visible in the painted lane. It is a tracking task.

The base observation contains visual speed, signed visual lane error, sine of visual heading error, left offsets at 3/6/10 m, stereo range, sine of bearing, a detection flag, and its two previous actuator commands. The steering network also receives three lane offsets estimated from previously observed markings. Both pure pursuit and the TD3 policies receive the same perception. The CARLA frontend uses native RGB, a straight-lane fitter and larger images. Both domains use the same metric visual-odometry implementation.

Speed comes from successive left images. Camera height and intrinsics warp pavement into a vehicle-relative metric grid. Three image patches are registered against the previous grid. Consistent displacements divided by elapsed camera time give forward speed. If that estimate fails, tracked pavement corners and a robust planar transformation provide a fallback. Feature loss can leave speed unknown. In training the missing channel is filled with zero speed, invalid progress is suppressed, and three consecutive invalid lane/speed estimates terminate the episode. At deployment, missing lane or speed triggers full braking. This estimator assumes a roughly flat road, calibrated camera mount and textured, static pavement.

### Offline learning and scoring

Each saved transition includes the camera observation, executed action, camera reward, next observation, terminal flag and teacher command. The reward credits visual progress and penalizes elapsed time, estimated lane error, steering changes and missing camera input. Visual completion adds 25. A visual hazard or departure costs 25. A timeout costs 12. For exact coefficients and the frozen protocol, see the [training study](td3-experiment.md).

Two critics learn discounted future reward. Their target is r + 0.99 (1 − terminal) min(Q′₁, Q′₂) for a smoothed next action. After each pair of critic updates, the actor minimizes imitation error minus a normalized predicted-value term. Three independently seeded actors provide the median steering and acceleration commands at inference. The longitudinal training data uses a 4.5 m lookahead demonstration controller. Steering retraining uses a 3.5 m lane-history lookahead and adds a squared camera lane-offset cost. Only the evaluator uses simulator truth to score progress, tracking and contact.

## Transfer to CARLA

The frozen ensemble drives a straight approach to a parked vehicle in Town10HD_Opt. Both domains share the 11-value base observation, three camera-history features for steering, and two normalized commands. The CARLA adapter converts positive-left steering to the simulator convention and splits the longitudinal action into throttle and brake. A deterministic supervisor checks range against 3 + 0.6v + v²/8 metres, where v is camera-estimated speed. Missing lane or speed, low disparity coverage or stale frames can also trigger full braking. The stop latches near standstill for this stationary target. The logged policy latency measures lane-history processing, the steering and longitudinal predictions, and their aggregation on frames where the camera inputs were available.

Fixed spawn poses set up the scene. After each command a separate evaluator reads reference depth, vehicle velocity, box geometry and map lane center. These references score the perception and trajectory. The single straight-road drive tests control transfer, while the held-out Gymnasium study measures route completion. ### Marked bends in Town10HD_Opt

Two separate 200-frame trials use road 1, lane −2 starting at OpenDRIVE station 38 m and lane +2 starting at station 116 m. Each uses the same frozen TD3 checkpoints as the straight approach. Curved-road perception projects the front image onto a level ground plane and scores pairs of bright stripes around candidate centerlines of the form `y(x) = b + mx + kx²/2`. The selected centerline supplies the current lane offset, tangent and 3/6/10 m lookaheads. History advances using visual speed and prior steering. Stereo depth and the existing brake checks remain active. The chase camera is recorded after control for visualization.

For evaluation, the vehicle position is projected onto a polyline sampled every 0.25 m along its assigned lane in the OpenDRIVE road coordinate. Lateral RMSE is the square root of mean squared signed perpendicular error across all 200 frames, including brake overrides. The distance is the sum of planar displacements, and the net heading change comes from unwrapped vehicle yaw. The horizon was fixed by frame count. These are two recorded passes of a single town curve.
